## 2. The core idea

**People get to know each other in two ways: face to face, and side by side.** Face to face is talking, catching up, one to one. Side by side is doing: dancing, working, cooking, building, volunteering. Most groups only ever get one of the two.

| If your people meet... | We organise... | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Face to face** (friends, mentors, remote teams, neighbours) | Something **side by side** | A build day, cooking for forty, a walk with a purpose, a day of volunteering, a first-aid session with accredited trainers |
| **Side by side** (colleagues, teams, dancers, volunteers) | Time **face to face** | A long table with no agenda, a story supper, a walk and talk in pairs |
| **Both, but only a little** | The one you have least of, then we alternate | A monthly rhythm |

**The third context.** What a group does together becomes a story, a running joke, a shared effort it is proud of. That belongs to the group, and it is stronger than either of the first two contexts alone.

**Not constrained to dance.** Dance is where we begin, because dance communities already share deep trust side by side. The method works for any group that knows each other one way and would like to know each other another, and a team can adopt it as a way of life: a rhythm of contexts, not a yearly away-day.

### Four design principles (fun infrastructure)

1. **Fun is a prerequisite.** Play lowers defences, and people learn and connect more when their guard is down.
2. **Hosts are architects, not preachers.** A host sets the frame, gives the prompt and steps back. People drive. Nobody is lectured at.
3. **Interaction is a must.** Every session has people doing, deciding or making. Nothing is passive.
4. **Continuity.** Every session ends with a way to carry on: a small toolkit, a ritual or a next step the group can run on its own. We pass the flame.

### Skills that stay

We weave enduring skills into the fun. We start with formats that need no equipment, and add equipment-based skills as partners join.

| Format | What the group does | What stays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Crisis communication and de-escalation** (no equipment) | Scenario roleplays on tense workplace or community situations | Listening, calm, quick negotiation |
| **Peer support circles** (no equipment) | Guided circles on noticing early signs of burnout and holding a safe conversation | Psychological safety, simple debriefing |
| **Decision-making and scenario mapping** (no equipment) | Groups work through ethical dilemmas and scarcity puzzles | Critical thinking, aligning different views |
| **First aid and basic life support** (accredited providers) | Hands-on, certified sessions | Certified life-saving skills |
| **Later, with partners and equipment** | Cooking and food preservation, basic carpentry and upcycling, urban growing, budgeting simulations, digital basics | Practical skills for daily life |

**Rules for skills.**

- Anything that carries risk (first aid, anything physical or health-related) is taught only by accredited providers. We are the cultural container and the convenor, not the certifier.
- We aim to work with organisations such as St John Ambulance Kenya and the Kenya Red Cross Society. **No partnership or endorsement is claimed until it is agreed in writing.**
- Peer support circles are peer support, not counselling. Hosts are trained, there is a referral route, and safeguarding applies.
- Accredited partners carry the accreditation. They do not carry our duty of care, which stays ours (section 13).

### General first, then curated to the industry

We begin with a universal format that works anywhere: a playful, tactile entry that gets people cooperating without small talk, a side-by-side task, a face-to-face reflection, and a toolkit to take away. Then we curate.

| Sector | The pain | The fun infrastructure | What stays |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Corporate and tech** | Silos, burnout, team building people dread | A crisis simulation hackathon with playful constraints | First-aid literacy (with accredited partners) and calmer crisis communication |
| **Schools and youth** (separate safeguarding and consent) | Passive learning, rigid hierarchy | A community resilience game, played like a live strategy game | Preparedness, peer support, shared leadership |
| **NGOs and civil society** | Burnout, echo chambers, hard stakeholder alignment | An empathy-bridge workshop disguised as a design challenge | De-escalation and lasting cross-sector trust |
| **Dance and creative communities** | Trust that never leaves the floor | The Lighting, suppers, build days, volunteering | A wider network of ways to know each other |
| **Spaces and neighbourhoods** | Empty shared rooms | A seeded community over three to six months | A neighbourhood that knows itself |

### How we avoid becoming another team-building company

| The trap | The Kandela move |
| --- | --- |
| The value ends when the room empties | **Infrastructure:** continuity loops, toolkits, rhythms and follow-up cohorts |
| Charging per head for one evening | **Programmes:** rhythms, hosted programmes, host training and playbooks, with measured outcomes |
| Buzzwords: synergy, vibe, growth mindset | **Hard credibility:** accredited partners and honest measures |
| Anyone can hire a DJ and rent a room | **A written method,** trained hosts and a community that compounds |
| A speaker preaching to an audience | **Hosts as catalysts:** people self-organise and drive their own discoveries |
| The annual HR checkbox | **A way of life:** a calendar of contexts |

The defence is the method and the evidence, not secrecy. We publish what we learn, including what doesn't work.

## 3. Platform

**Purpose.** To make sure good first meetings become lasting bonds.

**Mission.** We create the context a group is missing, make it fun enough that guards drop, and leave people with something that stays: a skill, a ritual, a way to pass it on.

**Vision.** A world where every group that gathers around something it shares also holds many other ways of being connected, and no first spark is left to fade.

**Founding story.** Candela is the measure of light: how brightly a flame burns, and in which direction. We bent the word with a K and gave it a heartbeat. We began with dance, because the body is the oldest lantern we own. We noticed that dance communities build deep trust that rarely leaves the floor, and that most groups, at work and at home, know each other only one way. So we made the missing context our work. The mission has not changed. It has gained depth.

| Value | What it means | In practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pass it on** | A flame loses nothing by lighting another | We give away tools, credit contributors and train hosts to outgrow us |
| **Point it somewhere** | Candela is light with direction | Every gathering has a purpose |
| **Gather, don't spotlight** | Fires gather, spotlights isolate | Roles rotate and nobody is the star |
| **Make it fun, make it real** | Fun lowers defences; substance makes it last | Play and a skill that stays |
| **Serve first** | Lamplighters give before they ask | Every Hearth leaves something behind |
| **Say what we know** | Much of this is still being tested | We report results honestly, including the disappointing ones |
| **Respect the room** | A good room runs on a light, unspoken understanding | Members are never the product, and consent comes first |

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Warm, steady, quietly confident | Loud, hyped, salesy |
| Playful, with a straight face | Silly or sarcastic |
| Generous and plain-spoken | Preachy or worthy |
| Curious and evidence-minded | Mystical or vague |
| Rooted, with a global outlook | Exotic, folkloric or "tribal" |

**Archetype:** the **Lamplighter**, who walks the street at dusk and lights the lamps for everyone. Part host, part connector, part caregiver.

## 4. Positioning

**Category.** Fun infrastructure for lasting bonds. We are not an events company, a networking club or a team-building agency, though we use events and serve teams.

**Positioning statement.** For groups that already know each other one way and would like to know each other another, Kandela Kulture supplies the missing context in a form people enjoy, and leaves them with a skill and a way to carry on, unlike one-off events that end when the room empties.

**Reasons to believe.** A written method (the contexts, the hosts' craft, the measures). Measured connection, reported openly. Hosts trained to run it. Skills taught by accredited providers. A ritual and a symbol people remember.

| Others | Kandela |
| --- | --- |
| Team-building days: one event, one context | A rhythm of contexts, and the third one the group makes |
| Networking clubs: face to face with an agenda | Time together first; whatever grows, grows |
| Social apps: scattered attention | Hearths: a few relationships, deepened |
| Dance schools: teach dance | Kandela: extends what a shared activity already built |
| Performance companies: sell shows | Kandela: performs as a front door, not the product |

## 7. Voice and tone

**Four lines.** Plain: short sentences and ordinary words. Warm: we talk to people as friends. Dry: a straight-faced joke is welcome, a forced one never. Honest: we say what we know and what we don't.

**Implied, not announced.** We don't announce rules we would rather people feel. The lightness shows in the invitation itself: no hype, no urgency, no agenda. The few rules that genuinely matter (consent, safety, privacy) are stated plainly, once, in the right place, not used as slogans.

| Situation | Tone |
| --- | --- |
| Inviting someone to a gathering | Light, brief, easy to say yes to |
| Welcoming a new member | Warm, specific, practical |
| Talking to a studio, venue or space owner | Respectful, concrete, benefit-led |
| Pitching to a company, school or NGO | Clear, evidence-led, modest about unknowns |
| Handling a concern or complaint | Direct, kind, accountable |
| Safeguarding and safety | Serious, plain, no humour |
| Campaigns and social | Playful, rhythmic, a little unexpected |

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| "Same people. Different room." | "Unlock powerful networking opportunities!" |
| "Bring a friend. Bring a dish." | "Don't miss out!" or "Last chance!" |
| "Good company first. The rest follows." | "Leverage your community for synergy." |
| "We're still testing this. Here is what we've seen." | "Proven to transform lives." |
| "Saturday, 7 pm, the courtyard." | "Join our vibrant community of movers and shakers" |

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| gather, carry, pass, host, light | leverage, synergy, monetise, ecosystem |
| connection, trust, side by side, face to face | networking, contacts, leads |
| members, Flamebearers, Lamplighters, Keepers | users, followers, audience (for members) |
| fun, play, practice, skill | gamification, engagement drivers |
| Hearth, Gathering, Kulture | chapter, event series, fanbase |

**Language rules.** Fire words (flame, spark, glow) are strong and tired: use them sparingly and prefer fresher angles (power cuts, stage directions, measurement, quiet observation). Rhymes are seasoning for merchandise and chants, not the voice. Never use terms for bodies or abilities as insults. Use the Swahili proverb as it is, with its meaning; any other Swahili needs a native speaker's review, with regional variation in mind. Claim only what we can show: no health, safety or outcome claims without evidence or an accredited provider.

## 9. Visual identity

### 9.1 The mark

The **Radiant Mark** is two mirrored hand-cut Ks, ash on the left and gold on the right, with a gold flame in the space between them and one ash and one gold dot on each side. The flame is not added to the Ks: it is what they leave between them. **Meaning:** two Ks are Kandela and Kulture and also two people; ash and gold show light passed from one context to another; the flame is the light carried between them; the dots are the wider network.

**Status:** final concept with a working vector set. A designer must rebuild it as the production master before any print or signage.

**The wordmark** is the refined hand-cut capitals, stacked (KANDELA above KULTURE), with the K in KULTURE in gold. It is the only wordmark and is never retyped in a font.

| Version | Where it is used | Colour mapping |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary stacked, on Night (default)** | Digital, signage, stage, key art | Ash for the left K, left dots and wordmark; Candle Gold for the right K, flame, gold dots and the K of KULTURE |
| **Primary stacked, on Ash** | Letterhead, print, documents | Night takes every job Ash had; Deep Gold replaces Candle Gold |
| **Horizontal, two-line and one-line** | Partner pages, banners, website header | Same colours as the stacked version |
| **One-colour** | Stamps, embroidery, engraving | Solid Night on Gold or Ash; solid Ash or Candle Gold on Night |
| **Night panel** | Photographs, mid-tone or busy backgrounds | The dark lockup on a solid Night rectangle |
| **Symbol only** | Social posts, merchandise, large marks | Two Ks, flame and dots |
| **Icon (small sizes)** | Avatars, app tiles, favicons, pins | Two Ks and flame only, no dots, widened flame gap |
| **Wordmark only** | Where the symbol appears nearby | Hand-cut wordmark on Night or Ash |
| **Hearth lockup** | Each local chapter | Mark plus the city name (**OPEN**: to be designed) |

**Light backgrounds.** Dark is the home mode. On light backgrounds Night takes every job Ash had, and gold deepens to **Deep Gold (#B87A00)**, because Candle Gold on Ash is only about 1.5:1. Use Ash, never pure white. When unsure, use the Night panel.

**Before the artwork is final (OPEN):** test the flame at 32 px and in embroidery (widen the gap if it fuses with the gold K); confirm the flame's lean reads as light moving from one context to the other; run a five-second test (people should say light, flame or connection, not fire alarm or gas company); rebuild as a clean vector and clear trademarks.

### 9.5 Imagery, consent and credit

**Direction.** Documentary, low light, warm practical light (candles, lamps, doorway glow). People side by side, hands at work, rooms and light. Faces turned away or softly out of focus unless consent is recorded for that specific use. No stock photography. Illustration comes from handmade-looking media (linocut, scratchboard, layered paper cut, textured painting).

**Image system.** The website and social channels are image-heavy. Every photo has a slot with a fixed shape, so nothing jumps while it loads, and a branded placeholder shows until a real photo is added.

| Rule | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| **Consent first** | A photo is published only when consent is recorded for it. Anyone can ask for a photo to be changed or removed, and we act within 24 hours |
| **Alt text** | Written for every photo, describing what is shown, never left as a placeholder |
| **Credit** | Every photo carries the photographer's credit |
| **Metadata** | Location and camera data are stripped before publishing |
| **Under 18s** | No one under 18 appears in content |
| **AI imagery** | Used for exploration and art only, clearly labelled, and never to depict real people or events. Final artwork for key marks and patterns is rebuilt by a human designer |

### 9.6 Icons, motion and layout

**Icons:** single-weight, hand-cut, ash and gold on Night. **Motion:** light travels from one point to the next. Headlines light up word by word, menus open as a circle of light from where you touched, and page changes reveal from the point you clicked. Everything is calm, never looping, and switched off for people who prefer reduced motion. Sound is off until asked. **Layout:** dark first with one point of gold; generous space; hand-cut edges; side by side compositions; directional light.

### 9.7 Social look

Instagram and TikTok use the Night and Ash templates, one gold accent per frame, patterns on clear plates and the icon as the avatar. Video is vertical, captioned, calm, and starts clean in the first second. Posts link out; we embed nothing that tracks people.

### 9.8 The website

The website is the home base: a multipage site (gatherings, Moments gallery, the method with its two-contexts diagram and chooser, Teams, Hearths, the Passport, partner pages, volunteering, safety, privacy and more), dark first with a light mode, fully accessible, with Instagram and TikTok in the header and footer. It is built for many photos, shown through slots, a gallery with filters and an accessible viewer.

## 10. Sonic identity

A single low heartbeat, a soft resonant chime on a touch, and hand percussion and voices building together. The logo sting is about two seconds and resolves on one held note. Music is live and organic, never generic stock beats, and licensed for commercial use. Silence is part of the sound: the Lighting begins in near silence.

## 11. The experience

### The arc of a session (general fun infrastructure)

| Step | What happens | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Enter through play** | A playful, tactile entry that gets people cooperating without small talk | Play lowers defences, and nobody has to perform |
| **2. Do side by side** | A shared task: cook, build, walk, rehearse, solve | Doing together builds trust faster than talking about it |
| **3. Talk face to face** | A short reflection, often over food | The conversations come back into daily life |
| **4. Take it with you** | A small toolkit so the group can run the session itself | Continuity: the flame is passed |

If a group already has the side-by-side context (a team, a dance community), steps 1 and 2 shrink and step 3 grows. If it has the face-to-face context, the reverse.

### Rituals

| Ritual | What happens | Where it shows up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Lighting** | A dark room is lit person to person until it glows. LED candles only, lit exits, capped numbers | Founding nights, flagship events, the Relay |
| **Lighting a Hearth** | A flame is physically passed from an existing Hearth to a new one | Every new chapter |
| **Kindling** | A newcomer's first gathering, marked with a small welcome | Onboarding |
| **The Relay** | Hearths light up in sequence as dusk travels west | Annual, once several Hearths exist |

### The member journey

Notice (a friend, a post, an invitation) then **Kindle** (a first gathering, kept easy) then **Return** (a different kind of context) then **Carry** (host, volunteer, run the toolkit) then **Pass the flame** (help light a new Hearth or bring a group).

### Safety and dignity

Written safeguarding and anti-harassment policies, known to everyone, with a clear reporting route. Gatherings are for people 18 and over unless a separate, supervised programme has its own consent. Accessible venues and adjustments for sensory and mobility needs. Dark-room events use LED candles only, lit exits and capacity limits. Consent for photography, filming and any record of an exchange.

## 13. Protecting the brand and the people

Building defensibly means protecting people first, then the name, then the work.

| --- | --- | --- |
| **Duty of care** | Waivers reduce liability but do not remove it, and an accredited partner does not take our duty of care away | Participant waivers and informed consent for physical or skill sessions (including photography and conduct), reviewed by a Kenyan lawyer; public liability insurance for every event; venue and safety checks |
| **Accredited delivery** | We never self-certify a risky skill | Written agreements with accredited providers before any first-aid or health-related session |
| **Safeguarding** | People first | A written policy, a trained safeguarding lead, a clear reporting route, 18+ by default |
| **Name and mark** | Protect before we scale | A clearance search, then filing for the name, the mark and the wordmark with the Kenya Industrial Property Institute (KIPI), with the classes confirmed by a trade mark agent |
| **The method and materials** | An idea cannot be trademarked; the materials, the name and the licence can be protected | Open core (principles, rituals peers can replicate) and proprietary packages (curricula, host training, playbooks sold to organisations, measurement templates) |
| **Partner agreements** | No handshake deals | An MoU covering scope, who delivers what, brand-use rights, liability split, data protection, revenue share, term and exit. Written permission before using any partner's name or logo |
| **Data and images** | Kenya's Data Protection Act applies, and images are personal data | Consent per purpose, registration with the data commissioner as required, 24-hour take-down, no sale or sharing of contacts |
| **Honest claims** | Trust is the asset | No claim of impact, accreditation or partnership that we cannot prove |
| **The champion** | A respected bridge between grassroots spaces and institutions (for example from public health, emergency response or community mobilisation) | A written advisory role with a clear scope, term and credit, so the work never depends on one person |

# Part B: Using the brand

*For partners, Hearths, volunteers, designers and press. Short rules in plain words.*

## B1. Ten checks before you publish

1. The name is right: Kandela Kulture, then Kandela.
2. The logo is an official file, unaltered, on a permitted background.
3. The colours come from the palette, with no Candle Gold on light backgrounds.
4. Headlines are in Bricolage Grotesque and text in DM Sans.
5. There is one pattern at most, and the logo sits on a clear plate.
6. Every photo has recorded consent, alt text and a credit, and faces are turned away unless consent covers this use.
7. It sounds like us: plain, warm, dry, honest, with no hype.
8. It claims only what we can prove: no certification, health or outcome claim we cannot back up.
9. Partners and sponsors are named only with their written permission, and paid partnerships are disclosed.
10. If it is new or goes beyond sharing what we publish, it has been approved.

## B2. Our name

| Write | Don't write |
| --- | --- |

| Hearth, Keeper, Lamplighter, Flamebearer, with capitals | chapter, host, volunteer, fan (for our own terms) |
| The Lighting, Kandela Rhythm, Skills That Stay | Our programme names attached to a partner's name |

## B3. The logo

## B4. Colour, type and patterns

Use only the palette in section 9.2, with the contrast rules there. Smoke is for Night backgrounds; Smoke Deep is for Ash. Headlines are Bricolage Grotesque in sentence case, text is DM Sans, never a filter or distortion on text. Use one pattern family per surface, tone on tone, and keep the logo on a clear plate. Do not borrow regional print traditions as decoration.

## B5. Photography and film

- **Ask first.** Tell people photos may be taken, give a clear way to opt out, and record consent for each use.
- **Faces away by default.** Show hands, rooms and light. Show a face only with recorded consent for that use.
- **Credit and describe.** Every photo has a credit and alt text.
- **No one under 18.** No stock photography.
- **Take it down.** If anyone asks, remove or change a photo within 24 hours.
- **Don't tag members** in promotional posts, and don't post a gathering's exact location live.
- **AI images** are for exploration and art only, labelled, and never show real people or events.

## B6. Words

Use the names in section 8 and the lines in section 6. Keep to plain, warm, dry and honest. Avoid hype, urgency and jargon such as synergy, leverage or ecosystem. Never write or imply "certified by Kandela", and never describe a skill session as accredited unless the accredited provider delivers it and says so in writing.

## B7. Social media

- Use the official handles and templates. Three to five hashtags at most.
- No engagement bait, no giveaways that ask people to tag friends.
- Repost a member's content only with permission and credit.
- Disclose sponsored posts with the platform's partnership tools.
- Move direct messages to WhatsApp. Anything about safety or harm goes to the safeguarding lead, not a public reply.
- Captions on every video, alt text on every image.
