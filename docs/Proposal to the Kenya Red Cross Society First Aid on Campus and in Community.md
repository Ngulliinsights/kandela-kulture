# Proposal to the Kenya Red Cross Society: First Aid on Campus and in Community

Nov 14, 2026 · @naam

We propose a pilot with the Kenya Red Cross Society that takes first-aid training to students through cross-college salsa tours, and leaves behind campus chapters that keep the training, and the volunteers it produces, going. Draft for discussion; nothing here has been agreed.

## 1. What we propose

Kandela Kulture creates the setting and the pull. The Kenya Red Cross Society brings the training, the certification and the volunteer register. Together we run a pilot across several campuses, then turn each campus group into a lasting chapter that can carry more of your programmes.

We do not teach or certify first aid. Your trainers do that.

## 2. Why salsa clubs are the way in

Salsa clubs are among the most social groups on a campus. They meet regularly, mix students across courses and years, and put on visible events. In our experience, their leaders carry real influence in their institutions. We treat this as an assumption to test, not a fact: in the first month we will map which clubs on each campus actually draw and connect students.

A tour across colleges adds something a single campus cannot. Students meet peers from other institutions, and your volunteer base grows across campuses rather than inside one.

Salsa is the first door, not the only one. Dancing is not required to take part in the training or to join the chapter.

## 3. The pilot: cross-college salsa tour

Each stop on the tour follows the same four moves.

1. **Spark.** A social salsa night or a short partner-dance game. People arrive to enjoy themselves.
2. **Pivot.** We move students from facing each other to working side by side in small teams.
3. **Layer.** Your trainers lead hands-on first-aid practice, such as bleeding control, recovery position and CPR basics, in teams, with a certified path for those who want it.
4. **Pass.** Each club leaves with a toolkit to run a refresher themselves, and students are invited to sign up to your volunteer register.

We suggest four to six campuses over one semester, to be agreed with you. The number is a starting point and will depend on your trainers' capacity.

## 4. Chapters: infrastructure, not one-off events

The aim is a chapter on each campus: a recognised club, formed with the institution's approval, that holds a small first-aid-trained core, a short code of conduct, and a regular rhythm of activity.

A chapter becomes a conduit for other opportunities. Once it exists, it can host refresher sessions, awareness days, service mornings and any programme you already run, without rebuilding the connection each time. This is why the pilot is partnership heavy: the chapter only works if the institution, the club and you are each in the room.

## 5. Wider partners

- **Other clubs.** Sports, drama, hiking and student associations join as hosts or participants, so the chapter is not tied to one social circle.
- **Faith and student religious groups.** They are invited as hosts and participants on equal terms. The programme stays open to every faith and none.
- **Estate fellowships (a second audience).** We read this as residents' fellowships in housing estates. They gather in a different environment with different stakes: not a service or a residents' meeting, but a practical skill with their neighbours. First aid fits, because neighbours are often first on the scene. We would pilot this after the campus phase. Please correct our reading if estate fellowships means something else.

## 7. How we will measure the pilot

- Students trained, and students certified.
- Sign-ups to your volunteer register.
- Chapters formed, and clubs and institutions engaged.
- Students who return for a second session.
- Clubs that run a refresher without us.

We will agree the targets with you before the first stop.

## 8. Safeguards and terms

- **Your name and emblem.** We will not use the Red Cross name or emblem without your written approval.
- **Instruction.** Only your trainers teach first aid.
- **Consent.** Every participant signs a participation and media agreement, and photography is off by default.
- **Data.** Personal data is handled in line with Kenya's Data Protection Act, and shared with you only as the participant agrees.
- **Written agreement.** A memorandum of understanding sets out roles, brand use, liability and data before the first stop.
- **Institutions.** Club registration and events go through each institution's own approval process.

## 9. Phasing: strategic and organic

We phase the work as gates, not a calendar. Each phase has to earn the next, and each new chapter is lit from one that already exists, so growth follows relationships and results instead of a fixed rollout.

1. **Ground.** We map the clubs, agree the memorandum and run one half-day trial on a single campus with a salsa club. It comes first because the risk is lowest and it shows whether the club's pull and your trainers' time work together. *Gate:* agreed trial results (attendance, completion, sign-ups) and the institution's approval.
2. **Light.** The tour proper, across a few campuses. Each stop is co-hosted by student leaders from the stop before, so every club is lit from the last. *Gate:* clubs can run a refresher without us, and chapters are ready to register.
3. **Root.** Chapters register as recognised clubs. Neighbouring clubs and faith or student religious groups join because chapter members invite them, not because we recruit them. Chapters begin to carry other programmes you choose. *Gate:* chapters keep going on their own and take on a second programme.
4. **Spread.** Estate fellowships and other community groups, brought in by members who live there, with safeguarding adapted for each setting and your trainers leading the training. *Gate:* a joint review with you.

**Principles.** Growth is pulled, not pushed: a phase widens only where students or members ask for it. Every phase leaves proof, such as results you can show the next institution. The pace follows your trainers' capacity. We can slow or stop at any gate. Dates follow the gates; only the Ground phase (about the first month) has a fixed time frame.

## 10. What we ask of you

A first conversation of about 45 minutes, a named contact on your side for training and volunteer sign-up, and a decision on whether a half-day trial on one campus can come first. We will bring the campus map and a draft memorandum.

## 11. Assumptions to confirm

- "Estate fellowships" means residents' fellowships in housing estates.
- Your trainers' capacity allows four to six campus stops in a semester.
- Each institution will approve the chapter as a recognised club.
