    A few people you may already know, and a host who looks after the room.

    Cook, walk, build, help out. The task does the talking.

    Easy company, new things in common, and no pressure to be anywhere you don’t want to be.

The first-timer guide

First time?

Your first gathering

You don’t need to know anyone, and you don’t need to be good at anything.

Overview

  1. Who comes

    Working people who dance, and sometimes a friend they’ve brought. Your host introduces themselves.

  2. What to wear

    Whatever you can move in. The invitation says if shoes or a dish are needed.

  3. What’s expected

    Arrive roughly on time, join the task, be kind, and ask before taking photos.

Come as you are

  • Nobody has to be anywhere or do anything they don’t want to.
  • Conversations, friendships and plans grow at their own pace.
  • If you’d like to stay in touch afterwards, you will.
  • Leave whenever you like, without explaining.

The signature ritual

The Lighting

A dark room, one flame, and everyone passing it on until the whole room glows. The audience is the cast and the ticket is the candle.

Click one light. Watch it spread.

How it works

  1. Darkness

    The room goes dark. LED candles only.

  2. One flame

    A single light is passed to a neighbour.

  3. The room glows

    Hand to hand, until nobody is in the dark.

Safety first

  • LED candles only, never real flames.
  • Lit exits, capped numbers and a safety plan.
  • An option to sit near an exit or in a lit area.

How it works

Know people the other way

People get to know each other in two ways: face to face, and side by side. Most groups only ever get one. We add the other, and the new experience becomes a third thing you share.

Two ways to know someone

Tell us how your people meet

How do your people know each other today?

The third context

What you do together makes something new: a story, a running joke, a shared effort you are proud of. That belongs to the group, and it is stronger than anything either of the first two contexts gave you on its own.

Team building, as a way of life

Not an away-day once a year, but a rhythm. One month side by side, the next face to face, and each experience adds to what the group shares.

  1. This month

    Side by side: a build day, a cook-together, a walk, a day of volunteering.

  2. Next month

    Face to face: a long table and an unhurried conversation.

  3. And so on

    Every new experience adds a layer, and the group keeps growing together.

Dance is where we begin

Dance communities already share deep trust side by side, so adding the other context is the lowest-hanging fruit. The same method works for teams, studios, congregations, estates and alumni networks: any group that knows each other one way and would like to know each other another.

What stays the same

  • It stays light, and it is always optional.
  • Everyone takes a turn: roles rotate and nobody holds rank.
  • Nothing is owed after a good evening.
  • You can leave without explaining.

Add a thread

Our hypothesis, still being tested: pairs who share three or more kinds of experience will help each other without being asked.

How we measure

We count connection, not attendance: how many different contexts each pair shares, what actually passes between them, and whether it would have happened without us. We publish what we learn, including what doesn’t work.

Why we exist

Light is meant to be passed on

The mission hasn’t changed. It has gained depth.

Candela is the measure of light: how brightly a flame burns, and in which direction. We took the word, bent it with a K, and gave it a heartbeat.

Kandela is about the small, stubborn flame: the one you carry into a dark room, the one you shield with your hands, the one that lights another and loses nothing in the giving.

We begin with dance, because the body is the oldest lantern we own. But a flame doesn’t stay in one place.

You don’t just watch Kandela. You carry it.

Looking after each other

Safety and safeguarding

This is a summary. A fuller policy will be published once reviewed.

  • Gatherings are for people aged 18 and over.
  • Every gathering has a named host.
  • Each invitation states access details: steps, seating, noise and light.
  • Dark-room events use LED candles only, with lit exits and capped numbers.
  • We ask before photos, and remove anything within 24 hours on request.

Raise a concern

Tell us, and a trained person will respond. You don’t have to explain more than you want to.

Plain language

Privacy and consent

We collect as little as we can, and members are never the product.

  • We collect your first name and phone number when you RSVP, plus anything you choose to share about access needs.
  • We send reminders only if you reply YES, and nothing else without asking.
  • We never sell or share contact details, and sponsors never get them.
  • RSVPs happen in WhatsApp, run by Meta, which may process messages outside Kenya. Call or email us instead if you prefer.
  • We delete RSVP chats about 90 days after a gathering unless you ask to hear about future ones.
  • You can ask to see, correct, delete or move your data, and withdraw consent by replying STOP. We aim to answer access requests within 7 days and others within 14.
  • This page sets no cookies and has no trackers. In this preview, fonts load from Google Fonts.

Questions

Good questions

Short answers. Ask us anything else.

Do I need to dance?

No. The gathering is about the task and the people.

Is it only for dancers?

No. Dance is where we begin, because dance communities already share a lot. The method works for any group that knows each other one way and would like to know each other another.

Is this a networking event?

Not in the usual sense. It’s time together: eating, walking, volunteering. People often end up helping each other, and sometimes working together, but that comes naturally.

Who is it for?

People aged 18 and over. Many are dancers, many are their friends.

Does it cost anything?

Most gatherings are free for members. Some, like a ticketed Lighting, may charge. The invitation always says.

What happens to my phone number?

It confirms your place. We send reminders only if you opt in, and never share it.

Can I bring a friend?

Yes. Tell us when you RSVP.

What if I need access adjustments?

Tell us. We reply with specifics, and adjust where we can.

What if something goes wrong?

Tell your host or message us. A trained person responds.

Accessibility statement

We aim to meet WCAG 2.2 AA and to test on real phones and with real assistive technology.

  • Keyboard and screen-reader friendly, with visible focus.
  • Text scales to 200%, with high-contrast colours in dark and light modes.
  • No autoplay, no flashing, and reduced-motion respected.
  • Forms with clear labels and errors that say how to fix them.
  • Every gathering lists access details and offers adjustments.

Tell us what’s not working

If something blocks you, message us. We’ll fix it or find another way, and we’ll thank you.

For teams

Team building, as a way of life

A rhythm, not a day: the context your team is missing, then the next one, then the one you make together.

Overview

  1. Meet side by side

    If your team mostly talks, we organise something to do together: build, cook, volunteer, walk.

  2. Meet face to face

    If your team mostly works side by side, we make time to talk: a long table, a story supper, a slow walk.

  3. Share the third

    What you do together becomes a story only your team has.

What you get

  • A calendar that alternates the two contexts, month by month.
  • Trained hosts who look after the room.
  • Honest before-and-after reporting on how your people connect.

What we ask

  • A named contact and a few willing people to begin.
  • Time, not a big budget: small and regular beats big and rare.
