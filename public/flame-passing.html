Pass the flame

A Hearth is lit from another by passing a flame from hand to hand.

This ceremony is how we intend to do it. The first ceremony will happen when Nairobi is stable and a second Hearth is ready.

Before the ceremony

A new Hearth doesn't appear overnight. It needs:

  • A qualified Keeper — someone who has participated in at least 5 gatherings, understands the method, and has the capacity to host.
  • A local community — an existing group that wants to try this. A dance community, a work team, a campus club, a street.
  • A commitment — the new Keeper commits to running gatherings for at least 6 months, with support from the originating Hearth.

The originating Hearth reviews the application. If it's a fit, they schedule the ceremony.

The ceremony

This is a real gathering, not a symbolic ritual. It happens in the new city, with the new community present. The originating Keeper travels with a small delegation: the new Keeper, and one or two Lamplighters.

The structure

  1. Arrival — The delegation arrives. The new community gathers. The room is set for a normal Kandela session.
  2. Spark — The session opens with a Spark task, led by the originating Keeper. The new community plays. The new Keeper participates as a participant, not as host.
  3. Pivot — The session moves through its structure. The new Keeper observes, participates, begins to feel the rhythm.
  4. Layer — A skill or task is shared. The new community learns together. The delegation watches how the new group takes it in.
  5. The pass — Near the end, the originating Keeper hands the flame to the new Keeper. This is literal: a candle, lit from the originating Hearth's flame, passed from hand to hand. The new Keeper accepts it, lights their own candle from it.
  6. Commitment — The new Keeper speaks to the room: what they will do, how they will hold the space, what they ask of the community. The community responds: what they will give, how they will show up.
  7. Pass — The session closes with the toolkit. The new Keeper now has the flame, the method, and the tools to run their own Hearth.

After the ceremony

The new Hearth is not alone. For the first 3 months, the originating Hearth offers support:

  • Weekly check-ins — the Keepers talk. What went well? What didn't? How to adjust?
  • Lamplighter shadowing — Lamplighters from the originating Hearth can travel to help with the first few gatherings.
  • Shared learnings — what the new Hearth discovers feeds back into the method for everyone.

After 3 months, the new Hearth runs independently. The flame has taken root.

The Passport

Once there are two Hearths, the Passport becomes real. A member of any Hearth is welcome at all of them. When you travel to a city with a Hearth, you message the Keeper. They introduce you. You walk into a room where you don't know anyone, but you're already part of the circle. The flame you carry is recognized.

This is how we intend to do it

The first ceremony has not happened yet. We are in pilot phase, building the first Hearth in Nairobi. When Nairobi is stable — self-sustaining gatherings, a trained Keeper, a Lamplighter team — we will invite applications for the second Hearth. The first flame-passing ceremony will be documented here, with photos and a story of what actually happened.

Until then, this is the structure we will use. It may change based on what we learn. The manifesto promises: we say only what we know. This is what we intend.
