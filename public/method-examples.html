Method in practice

What Spark, Pivot, Layer, and Pass actually look like.

These are examples, not recipes. Every group shapes the bones to what they need.

Spark

We open with play — a task that needs cooperation and little talk. Play lowers defences. People connect and learn more when their guard is down.

Example: Human knot

Stand in a circle, reach across, hold hands with two different people. Without letting go, untangle the knot back into a circle. It’s impossible alone. You have to communicate, adjust, trust. No one talks about their job or their background — you just solve the problem together.

Example: Silent line-up

Line up by birthday without speaking. Hand signals, gestures, finding a shared language on the spot. The task forces attention to each other, not status or titles.

Example: Mirror walk

Pairs face each other. One leads slowly; the other follows exactly. Then switch. You learn to read someone by body, not words. It’s awkward at first, then fluid — and the fluidity is the connection.

Pivot

We move people from face to face to side by side, or the other way round — whichever they were missing. This is where the new context begins.

From face to face → side by side

A dance community already knows each other on the floor. We give them a shared task: cook a meal together, build something, clean a space. They’ve danced together for years. Now they’re washing dishes side by side. A different side of each person comes out. The conversation shifts from moves to ingredients, from timing to technique. A third thread starts between them.

From side by side → face to face

A work team sits in meetings all day. We give them time to sit across from each other and really talk — no agenda, no deliverables. The work conversation they’ve had a thousand times now happens in a different register. One person mentions something they’ve never said at work. Another responds in a way they never would in a meeting. The room feels different. The relationship has a new shape.

Layer

We add a second and third context: a skill, a service, a making task, a walk. Each shows another side of the people in the room.

Example: Learning a skill together

The group learns to listen when things are tense. The teacher is an accredited provider. The skill is real — it can save a life. But the layer is that everyone learned it together. The next time tension comes up in the group, someone says “remember what we practiced.” The shared skill becomes a shared language.

Example: A making task

Build something small together. A shelf, a bench, a set of decorations. One person knows carpentry. Another doesn’t. They figure it out. The person who usually takes charge watches someone else lead. The person who usually follows steps up. Both see something new in each other. The object stays. The shift in relationship stays.

Example: A walk with purpose

Walk through the neighbourhood together with a question: “What does this place need?” Not as gentrifiers, as people who care. Point out resources, gaps, strengths. The walk becomes a map of shared attention. Later, when someone mentions that corner, three people remember the same thing. That’s a third thing you share.

Pass

Every session ends with a toolkit, so the group can run it without us. We are the container and the convenor, not the permanent centre.

What the toolkit might include

  • The Spark task (how to run it, what you need)
  • The Pivot prompt (what to ask, how to structure the shift)
  • The Layer suggestion (skills, tasks, walks appropriate to the group)
  • A simple way to close (a ritual, a check-in, a next step)

What remains

A skill they learned together. A ritual they can repeat. A shared story about what they built. The flame passes to them. The session ends, but the third context doesn’t.
