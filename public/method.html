How it works

Know people the other way

People get to know each other in two ways: face to face, and side by side. Most groups only ever get one. We add the other, and the new experience becomes a third thing you share.

Two ways to know someone

One place makes an acquaintance. Two make a friend.

Tell us how your people meet

How do your people know each other today?

The third context

What you do together makes something new: a story, a running joke, a shared effort you are proud of. That belongs to the group, and it is stronger than anything either of the first two contexts gave you on its own. Same cast. New set.

Team building, as a way of life

Not an away-day once a year, but a rhythm. One month side by side, the next face to face, and each experience adds to what the group shares. One night is a spark; many nights are a fire.

  1. This month

    Side by side: a build day, a cook-together, a walk, a day of volunteering. Give them a task; they’ll give you a story.

  2. Next month

    Face to face: a long table and an unhurried conversation.

  3. And so on

    Every new experience adds a layer, and the group keeps growing together.

Dance is where we begin

Dance communities already share deep trust side by side, so adding the other context is the lowest-hanging fruit. The same method works for teams, studios, congregations, estates and alumni networks: any group that knows each other one way and would like to know each other another.

What stays the same

  • It stays light, and it is always optional.
  • Everyone takes a turn: roles rotate and nobody holds rank.
  • Nothing is owed after a good evening.
  • You can leave without explaining.

Add a thread

Our hypothesis, still being tested: pairs who share three or more kinds of experience will help each other without being asked.

How we measure

We count connection, not attendance: how many different contexts each pair shares, what actually passes between them, and whether it would have happened without us. We publish what we learn, including what doesn’t work.
